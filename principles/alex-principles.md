# Alex Hormozi: First Principles

Merged from four corpus slices (YouTube, courses, books, newsletter/workshop/X). Evidence in `alex-sidecar.md`.

1. **Every business is limited by exactly one constraint at a time, and a list of priorities proves you have none.** A business grows until it hits a constraint, then grows no further. Find it by asking what the one thing you do that makes the most money is, then what if I just did 50X more of that, then why not. Pick one, fix it, then pick the next one.

2. **Judge a business on gross profit, never revenue, and measure acquisition as lifetime gross profit to CAC.** Published LTV benchmarks come from software where margins are close to 100%. Over three to one is the minimum for a profitable business, not the goal: below it you're absolutely screwed, and at three you're still not in a great position.

3. **The business that can pay the most to acquire a customer wins, so a fixed marketing budget is evidence of a broken model.** Advertising is an auction, and he who makes his customer worth more to his business than does his competition wins. As long as a dollar in returns more than a dollar, the limit is spend as much as we can until something breaks.

4. **Growth is financed by customers, not investors, which is what makes 30 days the real target.** 30 days is typically the amount of time any business can get interest-free financing, credit cards being the prime example. Ideally you make enough from one customer to get and service at least two more in less than 30 days.

5. **Do ten times more of what already works before doing it better, and better before new.** He refuses to let effort be the reason something doesn't work for him, because most strategies work, comma, with enough volume. The rule of 100 is 100 primary actions every day, 100 days in a row; the same hundred spread over weeks is roughly 1/42 of the daily volume actually required.

6. **Test one thing, per platform, per week, and never judge a channel on a tiny test.** Testing multiple things at once means you never know what worked, so you never learn. 300 flyers can't tell you anything.

7. **Obscurity is your problem, not the market.** You sell 0% of people who don't know you have something to sell. Businesses using one method, on one platform, in one specific way, mistake the slice they see for the entire market.

8. **Fix the product until referrals beat churn before scaling advertising again.** If monthly referral percentage exceeds monthly churn percentage, the business compounds forever with no acquisition cost. Shoot for 25%+ of customers from referrals first; scaling ads without fixing the product is the hamster wheel of death.

9. **Sell the outcome, and sell it at the point of greatest deprivation.** You sell at the point of greatest pain, not at the point of greatest satisfaction: solving one problem creates the next problem, and the offer lands at that moment of deprivation. Sell them the vacation, not the plane flight.

10. **Proof beats promise, and not all proof is equal.** The likelihood a promise is believed is based on your proof: a 4.4-star restaurant with 20,000 reviews beats a 5-star restaurant with one review. In-person beats virtual, live beats recorded, raw beats processed, show beats tell.

11. **Reversing risk is the number one way to increase conversion of an offer.** Free converts nine times higher than not free because free reverses risk, and guarantees are the back-end version of free. The power sits in the stated consideration, the or what, not the word guarantee, and the point is maximum net conversion.

12. **Never drop the price on the same offer; change what is in it, or change who you sell to.** You can offer something different for less; you just can't offer the same thing for less. The correct price is one in which you hear no more than yes, but you make more money.

13. **Value is perceived, and the value equation is a division, so drive time delay and effort toward zero.** As the bottom approaches zero, value approaches infinity, and latency persuades more than the magnitude of a claim. A tooth extraction that takes 60 minutes is less valuable than one that takes five minutes.

14. **Simple scales, fancy fails, and growth is subtraction as often as addition.** The number one reason people cancel memberships and subscriptions is overwhelm, so adding more destroys value. They cut a 35-person tech support department and churn and customer satisfaction stayed exactly the same.

15. **Everyone is replaceable, especially the founder, and a bad result is the system's fault.** If you have keyman risk, you don't have an asset, you have a high-paying job. If the trainee gets it wrong or confused, the checklist, not the trainee, needs fixing.

16. **State the facts and tell the truth, and if the truth isn't compelling enough, change reality so that it is.** Selling hot is a nice way of saying lying, and scarcity claims use the constraints you actually have. Give someone their money back if they ask, don't take blood money.

17. **You cannot lose if you do not quit.** Losing most of the time is expected: he has lost money on over 9,000 ads he personally wrote. Once a winner is found, scale it until the earlier losses shrink into irrelevance. Patience is still the fastest way to get to your goal.

## What he refuses

- "Never give away the core service at a discount"
- "Never drop the price on the same offer just to get someone to buy"
- "we don't negotiate with price terrorists"
- "Never lie to prospects about scarcity; use the constraints you actually have."
- "don't take blood money; just give it back and go get another customer."
- "Never judge a channel on a tiny test: 300 flyers can't tell you anything."
- "Don't fire your sales guy if you've got advertising problems, and don't fire your advertising employees if you've got a sales problem."
- "he would never blame effort alone when something doesn't work"
- "Never say 'I'm not techy' or similar self-limiting statements about a needed skill"
- "Don't get romantic about how you want to solve the problem."
- "don't convince someone against their will."
- "You never leave a lead in no man's land."
- "Don't take advice on winning from people who aren't winning."
- "You cannot lose if you do not quit"

## Voice

Short declarative sentences and blunt imperatives ("Actually raise your prices."). Rules compressed into slogans repeated verbatim across every format ("Simple scales, fancy fails", "losers congregate, winners isolate", "more, better, new"). Contrast pairs and reversals ("Focus beats intelligence, consistency beats intensity, action beats intention."). Exact dollar figures attached to every story, his own losses first. Hedges left visible rather than smoothed ("don't quote me", "there's no science behind the number, just his own estimate", "I don't know that yet."). Direct second-person questions mid-argument ("Does that sound reasonable?", "deal?", "fair enough?"). Page numbers of his own books cited. Plain profanity when a line should land.
